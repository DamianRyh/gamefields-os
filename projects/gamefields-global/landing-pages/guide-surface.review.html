How to choose a sports court surface?

We compare artificial turf, polyurethane, modular systems, rubber, concrete and sports paint: applications, selection criteria and Gamefields prices per m².

Short answer

Choose surfacing according to the sport, intensity of use, substrate and maintenance, not just the price per square metre. At Gamefields, each finish is laid on a concrete slab or a stable existing site.

Surface systems

Artificial turf

A natural choice for many football pitches. It provides a predictable playing surface and works well for larger facilities. System specifications, appropriate sub-base, drainage and infill are essential.

Polyurethane surfaces (EPDM)

Often used on multi-use courts, school facilities and basketball or volleyball courts. They allow coloured zones and markings for multiple sports. The full system build-up should suit the substrate and required permeability.

  • Standard (spray): Permeable SBR + EPDM spray system, min. 13 mm. Multi-use courts, schools and recreation. Service life 8–12 years. PLN 250/m² excluding VAT.
  • Standard+ (2S system): Two-layer 2S system, 14–16 mm, improved cushioning. Intensive use, Orlik. Service life 10–15 years. PLN 317/m² excluding VAT.
  • Premium (sandwich / premium EPDM): Sandwich / premium EPDM, high durability specifications. Facilities with demanding requirements. Service life 12–20 years. PLN 400/m² excluding VAT.

Modular surfaces

Prefabricated modular systems can be suitable where rapid installation, individual element replacement and intensive use matter. Their application should take account of the base surface and intended sports.

Rubber: tiles and mats

Stabilised rubber tiles (PN-EN 1177, PZH approval, R11 slip resistance) and continuous rubber mats provide cushioning; tiles are water-permeable. They are suitable where fall protection matters.

Concrete, asphalt and sports paint

Existing hard surfaces do not always need removal. If stable and sufficiently even, they can be renovated, painted or used as a base for the chosen system. First assess cracks, defects and drainage.

How to compare solutions?

Sport
Identify the main movements and loads at the facility.
Users
Schools and publicly accessible courts generally see more intensive use than private facilities.
Substrate
The condition of the existing structure may determine whether complete reconstruction is needed.
Maintenance
Compare not only installation costs, but cleaning, servicing and ease of repair.
Design
If colours and branding matter, consider them when selecting the technology.

Consider the surface as part of the whole court

Start with the sport and site. The surface should follow the intended function rather than be an arbitrary first choice.
