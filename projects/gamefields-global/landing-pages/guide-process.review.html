Court construction step by step

Building a court involves a sequence of decisions: from site assessment, design, sub-base and surfacing to equipment, markings and handover. At each stage I explain how it works at Gamefields.

Short answer

First the site and users, then the concept, and finally the surface and colours. At Gamefields, you see the concept and price in Studio 3D before we sign anything.

Seven stages

  1. Site assessment

    First, establish the actual dimensions, site access, terrain, existing surfaces and surroundings. This stage can already eliminate formats that are too large or unsuitable for the neighbourhood.

    site photos and dimensions are enough to start; the “Will it fit?” planner on the homepage shows which models fit.

  2. Identifying users and sports

    A school facility, residential sports zone and club court require different approaches. Consider the number and age of users, intensity of play and whether the space should also be used outside training sessions.

    eight models, from compact 1v1 arenas to 3v3 courts; we select them to suit the user group.

  3. Concept and geometry

    At this stage, the court layout, safety zones, access routes and equipment locations are established. This is the best time to compare options and decide whether one large court, several smaller activity zones or a multi-use solution is preferable.

    the concept is developed in Studio 3D, together with the price and PDF project sheet.

  4. Sub-base and drainage

    This is an almost invisible part of the facility that has a major impact on durability. The layer build-up must suit ground conditions and the chosen surface. Water must be drained in a predictable manner.

    12 cm concrete slab (C20/25) with falls and expansion joints, on geotextile and aggregate sub-base, with reinforcement mesh: PLN 283/m² excluding VAT.

  5. Sports surface

    The surface should be chosen according to the main sport, intensity of use and maintenance requirements. Price per square metre should not be the sole deciding factor.

    eight surfaces, from painting (PLN 78/m²) to rubber tiles with PZH approval; choose them in Studio.

  6. Equipment and infrastructure

    Goals, hoops, posts, rebound boards, fencing, ball-stop nets and lighting require appropriate foundations and safety zones. Their positions should be established before the final surface layers are installed.

    the arena structure with rebound boards and goals is a single modular system installed on the slab.

  7. Markings, design and handover

    Finally, sports markings, colours and visual identity elements are added. Before handover, check workmanship, equipment stability and conformity with the design.

    one year of warranty and ongoing renovation support after handover; manufacturers' technical sheets and certificates included in the documentation.
